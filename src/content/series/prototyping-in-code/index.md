I’ve always felt that the traditional UX tools are not capable in testing certain interactions when it comes to designing for games.

A pass in a football game, a squad merge, Pressure in shooting a gun, I could design all of them in Figma, click through them, hand one to someone and watch them nod along, But a game was never the click. It’s the pressure around it, that half second where your thumb is moving before your brain has caught up. None of the tools I reached for could put that on a screen. 

With AI assisted coding, I could build the interaction myself, in the engine, and test the exact experience a user would get. No spec to hand off. No waiting. Just me, closer to the behavior i want to ship than a design tool ever let me get.

So this series is me revisiting that handoff. How I stopped treating a prototype as a picture of the interaction and started building the interaction itself. First beside the codebase, and then, eventually, wondering if I could get inside it.

Part devlog, part argument, and a good chunk of soliloquy about the bits I need to figure out.
