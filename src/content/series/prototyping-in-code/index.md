For years I designed interactions I couldn't test until a dev got their hands on.

A pass in a football game, a squad merge, a transfer flow. I could draw all of them in Figma, click through them, hand one to someone and watch them nod along, But a game was never the click. Its the pressure around it, that half second where your thumb is moving before your brain has caught up. None of the tools I reached for could put that on a screen. And the moment I wanted to actually feel it, the handoff began. Write the spec, redline the flow, wait for a developer to build the thing I was trying to describe.

Then AI changed where that line. Suddenly I could build the interaction myself, in the engine, and test the exact experience a user would get. No spec to hand off. No waiting. Just me, closer to the real thing than a design tool ever let me get.

So this series is me revisiting that handoff. How I stopped treating a prototype as a picture of the interaction and started building the interaction itself. First beside the codebase, and then, eventually, wondering if I could get inside it.
Part devlog, part argument, and a good chunk of soliloquoy about the bits I need to figure out.
